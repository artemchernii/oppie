# A Lisbon MTD back office must sell speed

Research date: 4 October 2026. Written for a founder who is not an accountant. Plain English comes first; the precise findings, with sources and numbers, follow.

## The short answer, in plain English

**Yes, money is already moving, and a small Lisbon team can plausibly make a living here, but not by being cheaper.** UK accountancy firms already pay outside companies to do this exact work. One Indian-run outsourcer, Initor Global, publishes a fixed price of **£10 to £35 per client per month** for it. The firms then charge their own clients roughly **£450 to £800 a year**. The work exists because the UK tax office (HMRC) now makes self-employed people and landlords send it a summary of their income and costs **every three months** instead of once a year. That rule is called Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, "MTD" for short. It started in April 2026 for people earning over £50,000. It widens in April 2027 (over £30,000) and again in April 2028 (over £20,000). By then about **2.9 million people** are covered, and about **two in three of them use an accountant**.

The catch is cost. A bookkeeper in Lisbon costs about **£19 an hour** to employ, fully loaded, by this report's own arithmetic. Indian firms *sell* the same hour to UK accountants for **£10 to £13**. So a Lisbon team only makes money if each client takes very few minutes. That depends on good software plus a trained person checking its work. This project's own test on made-up landlord data suggested **5 to 29 minutes per client per quarter**. That is fast enough, but it is a guess on invented data, not a measurement.

**Where a small Lisbon entrant could win:** practices that are full to capacity at the quarterly deadlines, and that dislike sending client data to India. Sell them a fixed price per client per quarter, with no minimum term, delivered in UK working hours, from inside Europe. **The biggest risks:** urgency stays low until April 2027, because late quarterly updates carry no penalties in the first year. The founder is not an accountant, while the outsourcers that succeeded were founded by accountants. Several legal questions are still open. **Kill test:** if two or three real practices will not give you a paid trial batch before wave 2 in April 2027, stop.

## How to read the precise sections

Every number below carries one of three labels. **Sourced** means a government page, a professional body or a named independent publication. **Vendor** means a company that sells outsourcing or software, and so profits if you believe the claim. **Arithmetic** or **inference** means this report's own calculation or reasoning; none of these is a measurement. Currency conversions use assumed rates of **€1 = £0.85** and **$1 = £0.75**. These are not quoted market rates and should be replaced with a dated rate before any decision rests on them.

## 2.9 million taxpayers arrive in three waves, two-thirds through accountants

The thresholds and dates are fixed in HMRC guidance: qualifying income over £50,000 from April 2026, over £30,000 from April 2027, and over £20,000 from April 2028 ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-out-if-and-when-you-need-to-use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax)). HMRC has published two sets of head-counts that disagree. Its costing notes say **780,000** for 2026 and **970,000** for 2027 ([GOV.UK TIIN](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/extension-of-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax-self-assessment-to-sole-traders-and-landlords/making-tax-digital-for-income-tax-self-assessment-for-sole-traders-and-landlords)), plus **970,000** more for 2028 ([GOV.UK TIIN £20k](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/making-tax-digital-for-income-tax-self-assessment-reducing-the-mandation-threshold-from-30000-to-20000-from-april-2028/reduction-of-the-mandation-threshold-from-30000-to-20000-from-april-2028)). A newer HMRC dataset, reported by the press, gives **864,000 / 1,077,000 / 975,000, about 2.92 million in total** ([The Accountant via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/hmrc-expects-42-taxpayers-comply-114710992.html)). Real sign-ups favour the higher set: HMRC later said about **860,000** were in scope for 2026/27, and its original 780,000 was roughly 10% too low ([PublicTechnology](https://www.publictechnology.net/2026/08/28/economics-and-finance/hmrc-to-begin-automatic-digital-income-tax-enrolment-for-almost-300000-people/)). These are HMRC forecasts before exemptions and churn, not counts.

The same HMRC dataset says **65% of in-scope taxpayers (1.9 million) use an agent**, meaning an accountant or tax adviser acting for them ([The Accountant via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/hmrc-expects-42-taxpayers-comply-114710992.html)). In the first wave, **69% of MTD registrations (393,000)** were done by an agent ([AccountingWEB](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/tech/accounting-software/agents-do-the-heavy-lifting-for-mtds-first-deadline), seen as a search snippet only). HMRC's own cost note expects that "many customers will make the quarterly updates themselves", yet it also warns that agent clients "may see increased agent charges" ([GOV.UK TIIN £20k](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/making-tax-digital-for-income-tax-self-assessment-reducing-the-mandation-threshold-from-30000-to-20000-from-april-2028/reduction-of-the-mandation-threshold-from-30000-to-20000-from-april-2028)). Wave 1 behaviour so far contradicts the self-filing assumption.

| Wave | Taxpayers newly in scope (sourced, HMRC via press) | Cumulative (arithmetic) | Agent-represented at 65% (arithmetic) | Agent-filed quarterly updates a year (arithmetic, ×4) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| April 2026, over £50k | 864,000 | 0.86m | 0.56m | 2.2m |
| April 2027, over £30k | 1,077,000 | 1.94m | 1.26m | 5.0m |
| April 2028, over £20k | 975,000 | 2.92m | 1.90m | 7.6m |

The timing matters more than the totals. By the first quarterly deadline on 7 August 2026, about **570,000 had signed up and 436,000 had filed**. Roughly **290,000, about one in three, had not signed up at all**, and HMRC began signing them up automatically from September 2026 ([PublicTechnology](https://www.publictechnology.net/2026/08/28/economics-and-finance/hmrc-to-begin-automatic-digital-income-tax-enrolment-for-almost-300000-people/); [SJC Chester citing HMRC](https://www.sjc-chester.com/post/making-tax-digital-first-quarterly-deadline-passes-as-hmrc-urges-taxpayers-to-act)). In plain terms, about half of wave 1 filed on time (436k ÷ 864k, arithmetic). Year one is gentle: **there are no penalties for a late quarterly update in 2026/27**. From the following tax year each missed deadline earns a point, and at four points the taxpayer pays **£200, then £200 for every further miss** ([GOV.UK penalties](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/penalties-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax)). The inference: **April to August 2027 is when the squeeze lands.** Penalties switch on and about 1.08 million new taxpayers arrive in the same months. The first wave-2 quarterly deadline falls in August 2027.

On the supply side, HM Treasury's supervision report counts **34,352 accountancy businesses** supervised for anti-money-laundering by professional bodies, **60% firms and 40% sole practitioners** ([HM Treasury annual report 2024-25](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/693308125b5198836f304150/24-25_Annual_Report_.pdf), figures from a search summary). HMRC also supervises some accountants and bookkeepers directly, and those are not in that count. Spread 1.9 million agent clients across ~34,000 businesses and the average is **about 55 MTD clients per business** (arithmetic; the real spread is very uneven).

Whether those businesses are short of staff is the demand signal. The best 2026 figure comes from a seller of outsourcing. Advancetrack's May 2026 survey says **73% of UK firms turn away potential clients for lack of staff** and **69% have service lines at or near capacity**. It names MTD as a workload driver ([Accountancy Today](https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/2026/05/13/accountancy-firms-turn-away-work-amid-staffing-crisis-survey-finds/)) [vendor; sample size not published]. IRIS, an MTD software vendor, estimates **one to two hours per client per quarter "assuming the data arrives clean"**, or 200–400 hours a year for 50 clients ([IRIS](https://www.iris.co.uk/blog/accountancy/the-true-cost-of-mtd-and-how-the-latest-tech-can-help-you/)) [vendor]. Widely repeated claims such as "68% of practices outsource" ([Corient](https://corientbs.co.uk/blog/why-uk-accounting-firms-are-choosing-outsourced-bookkeeping-in-2025/)) or "79% outsource MTD compliance" ([AccountingWEB sponsored post](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/community/industry-insights/mtd-outsourcing-why-smart-firms-are-outsourcing-making-tax-digital)) are vendor marketing with no visible method. This report does not rely on them. **No independent survey measures what share of practices outsource MTD quarterly work specifically.** The strongest demand evidence is indirect: many suppliers publish prices for this work, and suppliers do not publish prices for work nobody buys.

Practitioner opinion is split. AccountingWEB forum threads discuss roughly doubling fees for quarterly clients ([AccountingWEB Any Answers](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/any-answers/mtd-pricing-0), snippet only). One columnist argues agents should be banned from filing quarterly updates at all ([AccountingWEB blog](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/community/blogs/amy-chin/ban-agents-from-submitting-mtd-quarterly-updates)). Some firms will push low-fee clients to file for themselves rather than outsource the work.

## Eight Indian-delivered incumbents already sell this, mostly by the hour

The market is crowded and mature, but almost entirely delivered from India. **QX** claims 350+ UK accounting-firm clients and 700+ accountants, with a London address ([QX FAQ](https://qxaccounting.com/uk/faq/)). **Outbooks** claims a 500+ team, **33,000+ self-assessments a year** and ISO 27001 ([Outbooks](https://outbooks.co.uk/)). **IRIS**, itself an MTD software vendor, runs a 600+ person team in Chennai that sells "MTD Services" to practices in UK hours, 9 to 5 ([IRIS Outsourcing](https://www.iris.co.uk/products/iris-outsourcing/)). Advancetrack, Corient, Initor, AcoBloom and Acenteus sell to the same firms [vendor self-descriptions and a competitor's listicle]. ISO 27001, an information-security certificate, is near universal. A **free 10-hour trial** is standard (Outbooks, Initor, QX, Global Integra) ([Global Integra](https://www.globalintegra.co.uk/free-trial-for-accounting-firms.php)), so offering one only matches the market.

Two gaps stand out. First, **almost nobody sells the MTD quarterly cycle as a per-client product.** Initor publishes one. Digital Tax Service, a Sheffield business, offers white-label sign-up, quarterly preparation and "overflow quarterly update administration during peak periods" but publishes no price ([Digital Tax Service](https://digital-tax-service.co.uk/mtd-accountants)). Second, **nearshore suppliers are rare.** Connect Macedonia sells UK-time-zone accountants from North Macedonia ([Connect Macedonia](https://connectmkd.com/nearshore-accounting-services-uk/)), and Accace has a South African case study ([Accace](https://accace.co.za/about-us/case-studies/full-outsourced-bookkeeping-services/)). **No Portugal-based supplier selling white-label work to UK practices was found.** Time zone alone is a weak edge, because IRIS's Indian team already advertises UK hours.

Software and AI shrink the hours anyone can bill. Receipt-capture and bank-feed tools are said to cut data entry by **60–80%** ([Briefcase](https://briefcase.so/blog/best-ai-bookkeeping-software-uk-accountants)) [vendor], and AI categorisation is sold at about **£20 per company per month** ([AccountsOS](https://accounts-os.com/blog/accounting-software-for-uk-practice-firms-2026)). The inference: a back office that bills by the hour loses as tools improve. One that bills per quarterly update, and keeps the time savings, gains. Consolidation also narrows the market. PE-backed groups are buying Indian outsourcers: Springline bought two in November 2025 ([The Finance Story](https://thefinancestory.com/indian-accounting-kpos-rushing-for-private-equity-investment)). UK roll-ups such as Azets keep buying practices ([Outsource Accelerator](https://news.outsourceaccelerator.com/azets-alextra-accountants/)). Large groups are unlikely buyers for a small supplier, which leaves the independent small practices as the target.

## The margin lives between £240 and £800 a client a year

What practices charge their own clients is advertised, not surveyed. One guide quotes **£40–£120 per quarterly submission** or **£15–£60 a month** for MTD, rising to £30–£120 a month bundled ([AccountingFirms.co.uk](https://www.accountingfirms.co.uk/blog/making-tax-digital-accountant-fees/)). Landlord-focused guides put it at **£75–£125 a quarter plus £150–£300 for the year-end Final Declaration, about £450–£800 a year** ([MTD.digital](https://mtd.digital/mtd-income-tax/mtd-cost/); [Latch](https://www.uselatch.co.uk/blog/making-tax-digital-cost-landlords-2026)). Before MTD, the same client's single annual return cost about **£150–£500** ([Sleek](https://sleek.com/uk/resources/how-much-do-accountants-charge-for-self-assessment/)). No ICAEW, AccountingWEB or other fee survey with MTD figures was found.

## A Lisbon hour costs £19; an Indian hour sells for £10–£13

Lisbon salaries for this work are modest. Indeed Portugal shows an average of **€1,419 a month** for an accountant and **€1,241** for an accounting assistant in Lisbon, from small samples of 22 and 12 reports ([Indeed](https://pt.indeed.com/career/salaries/Contabilista%20Certificado(a)%20-%20Lisboa/Lisboa); [Indeed](https://pt.indeed.com/career/assistente-cont%C3%A1bil/salaries/Lisboa)). One English-speaking role serving international clients was advertised at €2,000–€3,000 ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Job/lisbon-accounting-jobs-SRCH_IL.0,6_IC3192045_KO7,17.htm)). Portuguese salaries are paid **14 times a year**, and the employer adds **23.75% social security** on all 14 payments ([Boundless](https://boundlesshq.com/blog/payroll-in-portugal/)). A tax-free meal card of up to **€10.46 a day** and about 1% accident insurance come on top ([Verestro](https://mealvouchers.verestro.com/pt/rules/portugal/); [Multiplier](https://www.usemultiplier.com/portugal/cost-of-employment)).

The worked example below is **arithmetic on stated assumptions**. It assumes an English-speaking bookkeeper at **€1,600 gross a month**, a figure between the Indeed averages and the €2,000 advert. That gives €22,400 salary, plus €5,320 social security, about €2,300 meal card and about €224 insurance. The total is **about €30,250 a year, or about £25,700**. Assume about 1,800 working hours and 75% of them chargeable, both unsourced assumptions, and you get **about 1,350 billable hours. That is roughly £19 an hour of direct labour**, before management, software, office, recruitment or profit. **That is 1.5 to 2 times what an Indian firm charges a UK practice for the same hour**, and Doshi's £10–£13 already includes its own margin. A loaded Lisbon employee at about £2,140 a month also costs more than QX *sells* a full-time Indian accountant for (£1,500–£2,000).

So everything turns on minutes per client. Three cases, all arithmetic:

| Case | Hours per client per year | Direct Lisbon cost per client per year | Clients one bookkeeper can carry (1,350 h) | Gross margin per client at £300/yr (mid Initor "Full") |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IRIS "by hand" estimate: 1–2 h a quarter plus an assumed 2 h Final Declaration | 6–10 | £114–£190 | 135–225 | £110–£186 |
| Project test on invented data: 5–29 min a quarter, plus the same 2 h year-end | about 2.3–3.9 | about £44–£75 | about 350–590 | about £225–£256 |
| Indian hourly benchmark for comparison: 6–10 h at Doshi's £10–£13 | 6–10 | (sale price to practice) £60–£130 | n/a | n/a |

The middle row comes from this project's own throwaway test. A simple rule-based sorter plus an assumed human checking speed processed four invented landlords at **5–29 person-minutes per client-quarter**. The "blind" landlord, whose payees the rules had never seen, took 15–29 minutes. The test excludes onboarding, missing information, mortgage-interest splits and chasing clients, and **in real practices chasing clients is likely the largest time cost**. Treat that row as a target, not a result.

A survival sketch, also arithmetic: two Lisbon bookkeepers (about £51,000 loaded) plus a £40,000 founder draw (assumed) need about **£91,000 a year of gross revenue** before overheads. At £300 per client that is **about 305 clients, roughly six practices with 50 MTD clients each.** Two bookkeepers have 2,700 hours. At 10 hours a client they can carry only 270 clients, short of the target. At 6 hours they carry 450. **The business works at IRIS-like speed only if the price holds near the top of Initor's range. At the test's speed, it works comfortably.** The national market is not the constraint: 305 clients is about 0.02% of the 1.9 million agent-represented taxpayers expected by 2028.

## Supporting-agent status makes the work legal; four questions remain open

The most useful regulatory finding is good news. HMRC created a **"supporting agent"** role for MTD. A taxpayer may have one main agent and **any number of supporting agents**. A supporting agent can sign clients up, **submit quarterly updates** and keep records in compatible software. It cannot see tax calculations, file the final return or request repayments ([GOV.UK: Choose agents](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/choose-agents-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax)). That is almost exactly the back-office job. All MTD work runs through an **Agent Services Account**, HMRC's online account for agents; an organisation has one, set up by a director ([ACCA](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/technical-activities/uk-tech/in-practice-ezine-archive/In-Practice-archive-2025/September/Making-Tax-Digital.html); [ICAEW](https://www.icaew.com/technical/tax/making-tax-digital/mtd-guidance/how-agents-can-sign-up-clients)).

That gives two routes (inference). In the **white-label route**, the Lisbon team works as named users inside the practice's own software, and the practice remains the only agent HMRC sees. In the **supporting-agent route**, the Lisbon company holds its own agent account and the client authorises it. That route is legal and visible, so it is not white-label. Sharing the practice's HMRC login with Lisbon staff is the route to avoid. HMRC guidance on credential sharing was not retrieved in this research, so this is background knowledge to confirm.

Four questions remain open. **Anti-money-laundering (AML) supervision**, the UK registration that firms handling client money matters must hold: a 2018 ATT guidance note says a business working "entirely outside the UK" does not need UK supervision ([ATT FAQ](https://www.att.org.uk/sites/default/files/180306%20FAQ.pdf)). It is eight years old, and no current HMRC statement on offshore subcontractors was found. If registration is required, the cost is small: **£300 application, £400 per premises and £40 per person checked, then £400 a year** ([GOV.UK fees](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/money-laundering-regulations-registration-fees)). For two directors that is about £780 in year one (arithmetic). **Whether a Portuguese company can open its own Agent Services Account** was not established. **Data protection:** the belief that the UK treats Portugal, as an EEA state, as "adequate", meaning safe to send data to without extra contracts, is background knowledge that the research could not confirm on ICO pages ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/international-transfers/international-transfers-a-guide/)). A processor agreement is needed either way. **Portugal's reserved accounting acts:** certified accountants registered with the OCC, Portugal's accountants' order, hold exclusive rights over Portuguese companies' accounts ([CRN Contabilidade](https://crncontabilidade.pt/blog/contabilista-certificado-quando-e-necessario/)). Keying UK records is probably outside that reservation, but this is unconfirmed.

Professional rules put the burden on the practice. ICAEW guidance says disclosing client information needs client consent or another lawful basis, and should be documented ([ICAEW](https://www.icaew.com/technical/tas-helpsheets/ethics/disclosure-of-confidential-information-for-members-in-practice)). In practice, a firm using a back office should say so in its engagement letter. The practice stays the data controller, so a breach at the supplier is still the practice's problem ([AcoBloom](https://www.acobloom.com/blog/gdpr-compliance/)) [vendor]. Liability for penalties lands on the taxpayer and, through them, on the practice. The back office's exposure runs only to the practice, under contract. Professional indemnity insurance for a Portuguese subcontractor was not researched.

## Trust, not price, decides who wins the first ten practices

The buyer in a small practice is the owner. Their objections are documented: **data security is "usually the first objection"** ([QX](https://qxaccounting.com/uk/blog-data-security-uk-outsourced-accounting/)) [vendor]. Practitioners interviewed by AAT cite brand risk, clients not realising their data goes offshore, and the ethics of offshore pay. Those in favour describe "an urgent need for staffing", and one visited the offshore office in person before trusting it ([AAT Comment](https://www.aatcomment.org.uk/audience/members/accountants-argue-for-and-against-offshoring-work/)). The incumbents' content is mostly about handling these objections, which suggests the objections, not the price, are the real barrier. A European, English-speaking team in UK hours answers the ethics and transparency objections better than an Indian one. On security alone it does not, because the incumbents hold ISO 27001.

The cheapest credible trust signals for a micro entrant are **Cyber Essentials from about £320 + VAT** (Cyber Essentials Plus from about £1,499) ([IASME FAQ](https://iasme.co.uk/cyber-essentials/frequently-asked-questions/); [Connection Technologies](https://connection-technologies.co.uk/blog/cyber-essentials-cost-uk-2026)). Add a ready-to-sign data processing agreement, a named person, and an invitation to visit Lisbon. One pattern is consistent: **the outsourcers whose founding stories were found were started by practising UK accountants**, and their first client was the founder's own firm. Propact grew out of a Birmingham ICAEW practice ([Propact](https://propactglobal.com/about-propact/)). Intelligent Outsourcing was built "first for his own firm" ([Intelligent Outsourcing](https://intelligent-outsourcing.io/)). A founder who is a developer, not an accountant, lacks that shortcut. The inference: the business probably needs a qualified UK accountant as a co-founder, adviser or anchor client before strangers will trust it.

Channels are known; their costs mostly are not. AccountingWEB claims **120,000 registered accountants** and sells sponsored content ([AccountingWEB](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/advertise-on-accountingwebcouk)). Accountex London (May) and Accountex Summit Manchester (September) are the main shows. Both 2026 editions are over ([ACCA](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/technical-activities/uk-tech/in-practice-ezine-archive/In-Practice-archive-2026/March/Accountex-London-2026.html); [Accountex](https://www.accountex.co.uk/insight/2026/06/25/accountex-summit-manchester-2026-the-norths-biggest-accountancy-event-just-got-bigger/)). One unverified listing puts a London booth at £2,500 ([Vendelux](https://vendelux.com/app/event/accountex-london-2026/240da8b1-6c1a-424b-a6aa-f2da930acf7c)). Referral schemes in adjacent UK accounting services pay **£80–£150 per end client** or 10% of invoices for six months ([The Accountancy](https://www.theaccountancy.co.uk/affiliate-referral-programme); [Basis 365](https://www.basis365.com/accounting-bookkeeping-referral-partner-rewards-program)). Xero's advisor directory admits only accounting practices, so a supplier to practices cannot list there ([Xero Central](https://central.xero.com/s/article/Your-Xero-Advisor-Directory-listing?userregion=true)). **No published customer-acquisition cost, trial conversion rate or time-to-first-contract exists for selling to UK practices.** Those numbers will only come from the founder's own outreach.

### Kill reasons, ranked by how likely they are to bite

The first is **low urgency until April 2027**. With no quarterly penalties in 2026/27 ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/penalties-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax)), many practices will improvise this year and buy help only when penalties and wave 2 arrive together. The second is **price pressure from India**: Lisbon labour costs more per hour than India's sale price, so any work billed by the hour is a losing contest. The third is **the founder's lack of an accounting credential**, in a market where trust passes from practitioner to practitioner. The fourth is **policy movement**: the £20,000 threshold for 2028 is announced and costed ([GOV.UK TIIN £20k](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/making-tax-digital-for-income-tax-self-assessment-reducing-the-mandation-threshold-from-30000-to-20000-from-april-2028/reduction-of-the-mandation-threshold-from-30000-to-20000-from-april-2028)), and MTD has been postponed before (background knowledge, unverified). The fifth is **software vendors moving in**: IRIS already bundles its software with an offshore MTD service. The sixth is **practices pushing clients to self-file** instead of outsourcing. The seventh is **unresolved legal questions**: AML, an overseas agent account, data adequacy and Portuguese reserved acts.

## Verdict: money moves; the opening is per-quarter overflow for full practices

**Is money already moving? Yes, for outsourced compliance work in general, and specifically for MTD.** The evidence is a published per-client MTD wholesale price (Initor, £10–£35 a month), published hourly and full-time-staff prices from several Indian suppliers, one supplier claiming 33,000+ self-assessments a year, and advertised retail fees of £450–£800 a year. **What is not measured** is how many practices outsource the MTD quarterly update itself. Every figure claiming to show that comes from a vendor. Under this project's rules, that is enough to pass the "is money moving" test for the category, but not for a specific volume.

**Where a small Lisbon entrant could win** is narrow. It is not hourly bookkeeping, where India is cheaper, and not "UK hours", which IRIS already offers. It is a **fixed price per client per quarter, with no minimum term, sold as overflow around the four quarterly deadlines**, to independent practices of roughly 2–20 people that are at capacity and uneasy about offshoring to Asia. It is delivered by a European team in UK hours, with software that keeps each client to minutes, and with the time saved kept as margin rather than passed on as lower hourly prices. The supporting-agent role provides a sanctioned way in. The 7 November 2026 and 7 February 2027 deadlines are the low-risk moments to run paid pilots, before the April–August 2027 crunch.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. "Is there a market?" is settled: about 1.9 million agent-represented taxpayers by 2028, published wholesale prices, and firms reporting they are at capacity. The open question is narrower and measurable: **can a Lisbon person, with software, finish a real client's quarter in under about 45 minutes, including the chasing?** Below that line the unit economics are comfortable at Initor-level prices. Above about 90 minutes they collapse against Indian rates. Nothing published answers this. Only real anonymised bank data and real practice timings can.

The second implication is about who has to be in the room. The documented winners in this market were accountants selling to accountants. A developer-founder's real edge is the software that cuts the minutes, so the likely shape is a partnership. That means a qualified UK accountant who supplies trust and the first batch of clients, and a Lisbon team plus software that supplies speed. The deadline that decides it is concrete: secure a paid trial batch from two or three practices before April 2027, or treat the idea as killed.
