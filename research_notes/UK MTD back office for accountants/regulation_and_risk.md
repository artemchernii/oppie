# Regulation and risk: a Portugal-based white-label bookkeeping and MTD for ITSA back office for UK accountancy practices

Research date: 2026-10-04. This is research, not legal advice. Search budget was limited (several primary pages returned 404 or rate limits), so several questions are thinner than intended; each is flagged in its Gaps section. Items marked "background knowledge, unverified" were not confirmed against a primary source in this session and must be checked before relying on them.

## 1. AML: does a Portuguese subcontractor need UK anti-money-laundering supervision?

### Takeaway
Probably not, if it works only as a subcontractor inside the UK practice's own controls and does no work "in the UK" itself — but this rests on a 2018 professional-body FAQ, not on a current HMRC statement, and should be confirmed with HMRC or a supervisor. If it does need HMRC supervision, the cost from 1 December 2025 is £300 application + £400 per premises + £40 approval check per person, then £400 per premises each year.

### Cited Findings
- HMRC AML supervision fees, effective 1 December 2025: application fee £300 (one-off, non-refundable, waived for businesses eligible for the small business reduction); premises fee £400 per premises (half fee if added in the second half of the year); approval-process fee £40 per person tested for accountancy service providers; annual declaration fee £400 per premises, and "HMRC may cancel your registration if you do not pay your annual declaration fee on time"; fit and proper test £500 per person applies to money service businesses and trust/company service providers, not to accountancy providers — [GOV.UK: MLR registration fees](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/money-laundering-regulations-registration-fees)
- Small business reduction: businesses with turnover below £5,000 receive a £500 refund after approval — [GOV.UK: MLR registration fees](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/money-laundering-regulations-registration-fees)
- Accountancy service providers (bookkeeping included) must register if they meet HMRC's criteria; beneficial owners, officers and managers of accountancy service providers must pass an "approval check" (the fit and proper test applies to MSBs and TCSPs) — [GOV.UK: Register or renew your money laundering supervision with HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/register-or-renew-your-money-laundering-supervision-with-hmrc)
- That page says nothing about non-UK businesses or about whether subcontractors are covered by a supervised firm's registration — [GOV.UK: Register or renew](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/register-or-renew-your-money-laundering-supervision-with-hmrc)
- ATT AML FAQ: "If you work entirely outside the UK, you will not need to be supervised by a supervisory authority, but should check the AML requirements in the country where you are working." (document dated March 2018) — [ATT Anti-Money Laundering Supervision FAQ (PDF)](https://www.att.org.uk/sites/default/files/180306%20FAQ.pdf)
- ICAEW publishes a helpsheet on using the services of a subcontractor covering confidentiality, AML compliance, data protection and PII (seen only as a search-result summary; the helpsheet URL tried returned 404) — [ICAEW practice helpsheets index](https://www.icaew.com/technical/tas-helpsheets/practice)

### Inferences
- Plain version: the UK practice is the regulated firm that "owns" the client and the client due diligence. A back office that only keys in records and prepares quarterly updates for the practice to review is most naturally treated as an outsourced function of a supervised firm, not as an accountancy service provider in the UK in its own right.
- The risk case: if the Portuguese company contracts directly with UK taxpayers, or holds itself out as providing accountancy services in the UK, it looks more like a UK accountancy service provider and the "works entirely outside the UK" reasoning weakens.
- Portugal is an EU state, so the company will fall under Portugal's own AML law (EU AMLD transposition) as an accounting-services business; the ATT FAQ itself points to "the country where you are working". Not verified in this session.
- Worst-case cost if HMRC supervision is needed, one premises, two directors: £300 + £400 + 2 × £40 = £780 in year one, then £400 a year (arithmetic on the cited fees). Whether a Portuguese office even counts as registrable "premises" was not found.

### Gaps
- No current (2025–26) HMRC or OPBAS statement found on whether an offshore subcontractor to a supervised UK firm must register. The ATT FAQ is from 2018 and pre-dates later MLR amendments. Confirm with HMRC AML supervision or the practice's own supervisor.
- HMRC's "premises you need to register" guidance was not retrieved, so it is unknown whether non-UK premises are registrable.
- Portuguese AML obligations (Lei 83/2017 and supervisor for accounting firms) not researched.

## 2. HMRC Agent Services Account, authorisation, credentials, overseas agents

### Takeaway
MTD for Income Tax is only reachable through an Agent Services Account (ASA), and the client must authorise an agent in it. Crucially, HMRC created a "supporting agent" role that can sign clients up and submit quarterly updates but cannot see tax calculations or file the final return — that is almost exactly the back-office job. Whether a Portuguese company can open its own ASA, and HMRC's current position on overseas agents and on sharing the practice's Government Gateway login, were not confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- To sign clients up to MTD for Income Tax an agent needs an ASA and must have authorisation to act for the client in it; existing Self Assessment authorisations can be copied across from HMRC Online Services for Agents without re-authorising — [ACCA In Practice, Sept 2025](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/technical-activities/uk-tech/in-practice-ezine-archive/In-Practice-archive-2025/September/Making-Tax-Digital.html)
- An organisation needs only one ASA, and a director, owner or lead partner should set it up — [ICAEW: How agents can sign up clients](https://www.icaew.com/technical/tax/making-tax-digital/mtd-guidance/how-agents-can-sign-up-clients) (from search summary; page body did not render)
- A sole trader or landlord may have "only one main agent at a time, but any number of supporting agents", and may have supporting agents without a main agent — [GOV.UK: Choose agents for MTD for Income Tax](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/choose-agents-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax)
- Main agents can sign up / opt out, submit quarterly updates, finalise the tax position and submit returns, view calculations and amounts owed, request repayments and adjust payments on account — [GOV.UK: Choose agents](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/choose-agents-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax)
- Supporting agents can sign clients up or opt them out, submit quarterly updates, view due dates and use compatible software for records; they cannot view tax calculations, finalise or submit the final return, request repayments or view penalties — [GOV.UK: Choose agents](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/choose-agents-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax)
- Where several agents work on one business, "one combined update to HMRC is sent for each update period" — [GOV.UK: Choose agents](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/choose-agents-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax)
- Mandation thresholds: qualifying income over £50,000 from April 2026, over £30,000 from April 2027, planned over £20,000 from April 2028 — [ACCA In Practice, Sept 2025](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/technical-activities/uk-tech/in-practice-ezine-archive/In-Practice-archive-2025/September/Making-Tax-Digital.html)

### Inferences
- Plain version: there are two legitimate ways to do the work. (a) The practice stays the only agent and the Portuguese team works inside the practice's MTD software (Xero, FreeAgent, etc.) as named users, with the practice pressing "submit" or the software submitting under the practice's ASA connection. (b) The Portuguese company becomes the client's supporting agent with its own ASA. Option (a) is white-label; option (b) makes the back office visible to the client (the client authorises it), so it is not white-label.
- Sharing the practice's Government Gateway user ID and password with offshore staff is the unsafe route. Background knowledge, unverified: GOV.UK sign-in terms forbid sharing credentials and HMRC's agent guidance tells firms to give each staff member their own user via the ASA team management. Confirm against the Government Gateway terms of use and HMRC's agent standard.
- Background knowledge, unverified: HMRC's Standard for Agents applies to anyone acting as a tax agent, and HMRC's ASA creation flow historically required a UK AML supervision number for UK-based agents while overseas agents followed a different route. This matters directly to option (b).

### Gaps
- HMRC's current rules for overseas agents creating an ASA (eligibility, AML evidence, address) not retrieved.
- GOV.UK/Government Gateway terms on credential sharing not retrieved.
- HMRC's Standard for Agents and the 2026 agent-registration reforms (HMRC consulted on mandatory agent registration) not checked — may have changed by October 2026.

## 3. Professional body rules on outsourcing (ICAEW, ACCA, AAT, CIOT)

### Takeaway
The firm keeps responsibility to its client; disclosure of client information to a third party normally needs client consent or a lawful basis, and should be documented. Specific outsourcing rules, especially for offshore outsourcing, were not retrieved from the bodies' own pages in this session.

### Cited Findings
- ICAEW's confidentiality helpsheet: disclosure is permitted where authorised by the client (or a person with authority) and not prohibited by law; members should normally seek client consent, and document their considerations, including notes of consent received — [ICAEW: Disclosure of confidential information (members in practice)](https://www.icaew.com/technical/tas-helpsheets/ethics/disclosure-of-confidential-information-for-members-in-practice) (search summary)
- ICAEW has a UK GDPR client-files helpsheet — [ICAEW: UK GDPR](https://www.icaew.com/technical/tas-helpsheets/practice/gdpr-client-files) (not read)
- ICAEW subcontractor helpsheet exists and covers confidentiality, AML, data protection and PII — [ICAEW practice helpsheets](https://www.icaew.com/technical/tas-helpsheets/practice) (search summary only)

### Inferences
- Plain version: a practice that uses the back office should tell its clients in the engagement letter that work may be done by a subcontractor, including outside the UK. If practices refuse to say that, "white-label" turns into a confidentiality breach risk for them, which is a sales objection for the back office.
- Background knowledge, unverified: Professional Conduct in Relation to Taxation (PCRT, jointly issued by ICAEW, ACCA, AAT, CIOT, ATT and others) and the ACCA Rulebook's engagement-letter provisions expect disclosure of subcontracting. Confirm by reading PCRT and each body's engagement-letter templates.

### Gaps
- ACCA, AAT and CIOT outsourcing guidance not retrieved.
- No body-level ban on offshoring was found, but none was searched thoroughly either.

## 4. Data protection: UK GDPR and Portugal

### Takeaway
Not verified this session. The ICO and GOV.UK adequacy pages tried returned 404 or did not cover the point.

### Cited Findings
- ICO's international-transfers introductory guide (dated 15 January 2026) explains restricted transfers but does not list adequate countries; it points to its detailed guidance — [ICO: International transfers — a guide](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/international-transfers/international-transfers-a-guide/)

### Inferences
- Background knowledge, unverified: the UK has treated EEA states, including Portugal, as adequate since Brexit under transitional provisions of the Data Protection Act 2018 (Schedule 21), so transfers to Portugal need no IDTA or Addendum. The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 replaced the adequacy test with a "data protection test" but is not known to have changed the EEA's status. Separately, the EU renewed the UK's adequacy decision in late 2025. Confirm all three points.
- Even with adequacy, the Portuguese company is a processor for the practice, so a UK GDPR Article 28 processor agreement is needed regardless (background knowledge; confirm).
- Practices commonly ask suppliers for Cyber Essentials / Cyber Essentials Plus or ISO 27001; no source on what UK practices actually require was found.

### Gaps
- Primary confirmation of Portugal's adequacy status under UK law and any review date.
- Practice expectations on cyber certification; data residency of the practice's cloud software (data stays in Xero etc., which reduces transfer exposure).

## 5. Liability, insurance and the MTD penalty regime

### Takeaway
HMRC penalties land on the taxpayer, and in practice the agent who missed the deadline answers to the taxpayer; the back office's liability runs only to the practice under contract. There are no penalty points for late quarterly updates in 2026/27, so the first year in which a back office's mistake costs a client money for a late update is 2027/28.

### Cited Findings
- From tax years after 2026/27, each missed quarterly update or return deadline earns one point; at 4 points a £200 penalty, plus £200 for each further missed deadline — [GOV.UK: Penalties for MTD for Income Tax](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/penalties-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax)
- "There are no penalties for missing a quarterly update deadline for the 2026 to 2027 tax year", but the updates must still be sent before the annual return can be completed — [GOV.UK: Penalties for MTD for Income Tax](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/penalties-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax)
- Late payment: nothing up to 15 days; 3–4% of tax owed at 16–30 days (depending on tax year); from day 31 a further charge at an annual rate of 10%, daily — [GOV.UK: Penalties for MTD for Income Tax](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/penalties-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax)
- Secondary: the soft landing does not cover the final declaration (2026/27 due 31 January 2028, standard SA penalties) or late payment — [Xero guide](https://www.xero.com/uk/guides/mtd-for-income-tax-penalties/)

### Inferences
- Plain version: in year one the cost of a late quarterly update is zero penalties, so practices may not value a back office for "deadline insurance" until 2027/28. From then, four misses cost the client £200, then £200 each time.
- PI insurance: no source retrieved. A Portuguese insurer's policy may need to cover UK-law claims; practices will likely ask for a minimum cover figure.

### Gaps
- PII requirements for subcontractors (UK bodies set minimums for their members; nothing for unregulated subcontractors found).
- Whether HMRC can hold an agent (rather than the taxpayer) liable — e.g. agent dishonesty penalties — not checked.

## 6. Portuguese side

### Takeaway
Thin. Only secondary sources found: Portugal reserves "organised accounting" for Portuguese entities to OCC-registered certified accountants (contabilistas certificados). Whether that reserve extends to bookkeeping for UK clients was not established — the OCC statute PDF could not be parsed.

### Cited Findings
- Only OCC-registered professionals may perform external accounting functions in Portugal, and companies with organised accounting must appoint a certified accountant — [CRN Contabilidade blog](https://crncontabilidade.pt/blog/contabilista-certificado-quando-e-necessario/) (secondary)
- OCC statute 2024 (primary, not parsed) — [Estatuto da OCC 2024 (PDF)](https://www.occ.pt/sites/default/files/public/2024-02/Estatuto2024_Web.pdf)

### Inferences
- Plain version: the OCC's reserve is about who signs Portuguese companies' accounts for the Portuguese tax authority. Keying UK records for UK practices is probably outside it, but a Portuguese lawyer or the OCC should confirm. Unverified.
- Background knowledge, unverified: B2B services to a UK business are normally taxed where the customer is (general place-of-supply rule, CIVA art. 6(6)(a)), so invoices go out without Portuguese VAT, and the UK practice reverse-charges UK VAT. The company itself still needs its own certified accountant for its Portuguese books.

### Gaps
- Company formation (Lda / Unipessoal Lda, Empresa na Hora), Segurança Social rates, employment law — not researched.
- OCC reserved acts (statute art. 10) — needs a readable copy.

## 7. Kill reasons

### Takeaway
Two are sourced; the rest are reasoning. The sourced ones: in year one the regime carries no late-update penalties, and the threshold schedule (£50k, £30k, £20k) is the demand curve, so any further delay or threshold change moves demand directly.

### Cited Findings
- Thresholds £50k (April 2026), £30k (April 2027), £20k planned (April 2028) — [ACCA In Practice, Sept 2025](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/technical-activities/uk-tech/in-practice-ezine-archive/In-Practice-archive-2025/September/Making-Tax-Digital.html)
- No quarterly-update penalties in 2026/27 — [GOV.UK: Penalties](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/penalties-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax)
- The supporting-agent role lets any number of other agents submit quarterly updates for a client — [GOV.UK: Choose agents](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/choose-agents-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax)

### Inferences
- Regulatory delay: background knowledge, unverified — MTD for ITSA was postponed several times (originally 2018, then 2023, 2024, then phased from 2026). The £20k step for 2028 is "planned" and could move.
- The supporting-agent role helps the model (it is a sanctioned way in) but also helps rivals, including software vendors' in-house services.
- Practices' clients blocking offshoring, AI bookkeeping removing manual entry, and low-cost competition from India and the Philippines are plausible kill reasons, but no source on them was found in this session.

### Gaps
- No evidence collected on offshoring bans in client contracts, AI automation rates or Indian back-office pricing for MTD work.
