# Market size, timing and demand: white-label MTD for Income Tax back office for UK accountancy practices

Research date: 2026-10-04. Status labels used below: **[primary]** = GOV.UK/HMRC text read directly; **[HMRC via press]** = HMRC figure reported by trade press, the HMRC document itself not read; **[snippet]** = taken from a search-result summary, the page not fetched (treat as lower confidence); **[vendor]** = published by a software or outsourcing vendor with a commercial interest.

## 1. MTD for Income Tax waves: thresholds, dates and how many taxpayers are in each

### Takeaway
The three waves are April 2026 (qualifying income >£50k), April 2027 (>£30k) and April 2028 (>£20k). HMRC has published two sets of numbers that do not match. The September 2025 impact note says 780,000 / 970,000 / 970,000. A later HMRC dataset (August 2025) says 864,000 / 1,077,000 / 975,000, about 2.92m in total. Real sign-up data (about 860,000 in scope for 2026/27) matches the higher set, so ~2.9m is the better working total.

### Cited Findings
- Thresholds and dates: April 2026 for total qualifying income over £50,000 in 2024/25; April 2027 over £30,000 in 2025/26; April 2028 over £20,000 in 2026/27 — [ByteStart, summarising HMRC](https://www.bytestart.co.uk/news-insights/864000-sole-traders-and-landlords-face-new-mtd-reporting-rules-from-april-2026/); threshold mechanics confirmed by [GOV.UK: Find out if and when you need to use MTD for Income Tax](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-out-if-and-when-you-need-to-use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax).
- **[primary]** HMRC policy paper/TIIN "MTD for ITSA for sole traders and landlords", updated 2 September 2025: "around 780,000 people" from April 2026 (>£50k) and "a further 970,000" from April 2027 (>£30k), ~1.75m above £30k in total. One-off transitional costs ~£561m (avg £320 per business); net continuing costs ~£196m a year (avg £110) — [GOV.UK TIIN](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/extension-of-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax-self-assessment-to-sole-traders-and-landlords/making-tax-digital-for-income-tax-self-assessment-for-sole-traders-and-landlords).
- **[primary]** HMRC TIIN "Reduction of the mandation threshold from £30,000 to £20,000 from April 2028", published 24 March 2026: brings "approximately 970,000 additional individuals into scope". Transitional cost £380m; net continuing cost £101m a year. Exchequer yield rises from +£50m (2026-27) to +£975m (2030-31). This was announced at Spring Statement 2025 — [GOV.UK TIIN £20k](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/making-tax-digital-for-income-tax-self-assessment-reducing-the-mandation-threshold-from-30000-to-20000-from-april-2028/reduction-of-the-mandation-threshold-from-30000-to-20000-from-april-2028).
- **[HMRC via press]** A separate HMRC dataset (built on 2023/24 ITSA returns, reported August 2025) gives: April 2026 864,000 (30%); April 2027 1,077,000 (37%); April 2028 975,000 (33%). Total = 2,916,000. These replace an earlier HMRC estimate of 2.7m. The 2.9m is 42% of the 7,020,000 self-employed and property-income ITSA taxpayers in 2023/24 — [The Accountant via Yahoo Finance, 20 Aug 2025](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/hmrc-expects-42-taxpayers-comply-114710992.html); [ICAEW: "Up to 3 million individuals may need to comply with MTD" (Aug 2025)](https://www.icaew.com/insights/tax-news/2025/aug-2025/up-to-3-million-individuals-may-need-to-comply-with-mtd) (page fetched but only navigation rendered; headline and search summary only).
- Press commonly repeats "2.9 million sole traders and landlords by 2028" — [PR Newswire, Your Ecommerce Accountant](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/your-ecommerce-accountant--mtd-rollout-expands-to-2-9-million-sole-traders-and-landlords-by-2028--302870369.html) (press repetition, not primary).
- **Real-world check**: HMRC said ~860,000 people were in scope for 2026/27, and PublicTechnology notes HMRC's initial estimate of 780,000 was roughly 10% too low — [PublicTechnology, 28 Aug 2026](https://www.publictechnology.net/2026/08/28/economics-and-finance/hmrc-to-begin-automatic-digital-income-tax-enrolment-for-almost-300000-people/).

### Inferences
- **Reconciling the two sets.** The TIINs (780k / 970k / 970k, ~2.72m total) are the costing documents and appear to use an older base year. The 864k / 1,077k / 975k set (~2.92m) is newer (2023/24 returns). HMRC's own live figure of ~860k for wave 1 matches it. The "970,000 in 2027" (TIIN) and "975,000 in 2028" (newer dataset) are different waves that happen to be similar numbers. Do not read them as the same figure.
- Planning base: ~0.86m in wave 1, cumulative ~1.94m by April 2027 and ~2.92m by April 2028. These are HMRC estimates before exemptions, cessations and income churn. They are forecasts, not counts.
- The "2.7m earlier estimate" is about what the two TIINs add up to (780k + 970k + 970k = 2.72m), which supports this reading.

### Gaps
- I could not read the HMRC document that contains the 864k / 1,077k / 975k breakdown (likely a statistics release or an agent-facing data pack). ICAEW pages rendered only navigation. Its exact title and URL are **not found**.
- No HMRC split between sole traders and landlords per wave was found.

## 2. Share of in-scope taxpayers who use an accountant or agent

### Takeaway
About two-thirds use an agent. HMRC's dataset says 65% of the ~2.9m in-scope taxpayers (1.9m) are agent-represented. In the first wave, 69% of MTD registrations were done by an agent. Across all of Self Assessment, agents file 59% of returns.

### Cited Findings
- **[HMRC via press]** Of in-scope MTD taxpayers: 1,900,000 (65%) agent-represented; 1,018,000 (35%) unrepresented. 527,000 (28%) of agent-represented taxpayers did not file their 2023/24 return via software — [The Accountant via Yahoo Finance, Aug 2025](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/hmrc-expects-42-taxpayers-comply-114710992.html).
- **[HMRC via press]** HMRC data given to AccountingWEB (August 2026): ~69% of MTD registrations (393,000) were completed by an agent or representative, and ~31% (176,000) by taxpayers directly — [AccountingWEB, "Agents do the heavy lifting for MTD's first deadline"](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/tech/accounting-software/agents-do-the-heavy-lifting-for-mtds-first-deadline) **[snippet]**; page returned 403.
- **[HMRC via press, snippet]** Self Assessment 2024/25 (31 Jan 2025 deadline): 59% (6.6m) of returns filed by agents. The share has drifted slightly down in percentage terms since 2020. 89% of agent filings used specialist third-party software; 97% (4.5m) of unrepresented filers used HMRC's portal — [AccountingWEB: SA stats flag digital tax challenges ahead](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/tech/accounting-software/self-assessment-stats-flag-digital-tax-challenges-ahead) (403, snippet only).
- **[primary]** HMRC's £20k TIIN says agent clients "may see increased agent charges, especially where the agent completes the MTD quarterly update". It also expects "many customers will make the quarterly updates themselves, without agent assistance" — [GOV.UK TIIN £20k](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/making-tax-digital-for-income-tax-self-assessment-reducing-the-mandation-threshold-from-30000-to-20000-from-april-2028/reduction-of-the-mandation-threshold-from-30000-to-20000-from-april-2028).
- **[snippet, single-practice anecdote]** One practice with 50+ MTD clients reported 65% wanted the accountant to file the quarterly updates, 23% wanted help with set-up and the first update only, and 12% chose DIY — surfaced via AccountingWEB community search results ([AccountingWEB "MTD Pricing" Any Answers](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/any-answers/mtd-pricing-0)); exact page attribution unverified.

### Inferences
- Agent-represented pool: ~0.56m in wave 1 (65% × 864k), ~1.26m cumulative by 2027, ~1.9m by 2028. That is 4 quarterly updates a year, so roughly 2.2m, 5.0m and 7.6m agent-filed quarterly submissions a year. This is derived arithmetic, not an HMRC figure.
- HMRC's working assumption that many taxpayers will self-file quarterly conflicts with what happened in wave 1 (69% of registrations agent-led). Agent share may be higher still for the lower-income 2027/2028 waves, which are more likely to be landlords with simple affairs. That is unverified either way.

### Gaps
- No HMRC split of *quarterly updates* (as opposed to registrations) filed by agents vs directly was found.
- No MTD-specific agent share by wave (2027/2028 cohorts) was found.

## 3. Sign-up, readiness, first deadline and penalties

### Takeaway
By the first quarterly deadline (7 Aug 2026), about 570,000 of ~860,000 had signed up and 436,000 had filed. About 290,000 (one in three) had not signed up, and HMRC began signing them up automatically from September 2026. There are no penalty points for late quarterly updates in 2026/27. Points-based penalties start from April 2027.

### Cited Findings
- First quarterly update (6 Apr–5 Jul 2026, or calendar quarter 1 Apr–30 Jun) due 7 August 2026 — [salarytax.uk](https://salarytax.uk/news/mtd-itsa-first-quarterly-deadline-august-2026); [Together Accounting](https://www.togetheraccounting.co.uk/blog/mtd-quarterly-update-august-2026/).
- **[HMRC via press]** As of ~12 Aug 2026: more than 570,000 registered; 436,000 filed a first quarterly update by 7 Aug; ~294,000 mandated taxpayers not signed up — [SJC Chester, citing HMRC](https://www.sjc-chester.com/post/making-tax-digital-first-quarterly-deadline-passes-as-hmrc-urges-taxpayers-to-act) (no HMRC URL given). Same figures: [AccountingWEB "HMRC to start signing up MTD no-shows"](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/tax/hmrc-policy/hmrc-to-start-signing-up-mtd-no-shows) **[snippet]**.
- **[HMRC via press]** ~860,000 in scope for 2026/27; 570,000 signed up; ~290,000 (one in three) yet to sign up. Auto-enrolment starts September 2026 at "thousands per day" and pauses around the 31 January SA peak. About 6,500 exemption applications by June, ~4,000 decided, ~3,000 granted, ~1,000 refused; 40+ FTE staff on exemptions; 50+ recognised software products from ~40 publishers, ~20 free. Quote from Craig Ogilvie, HMRC Director of MTD — [PublicTechnology, 28 Aug 2026](https://www.publictechnology.net/2026/08/28/economics-and-finance/hmrc-to-begin-automatic-digital-income-tax-enrolment-for-almost-300000-people/); also [ICAS](https://www.icas.com/news-insights-events/news/tax/hmrc-to-sign-up-taxpayers-for-mtd-automatically-from-september) **[snippet]**.
- Penalty soft landing: no penalty points for late quarterly updates in 2026/27. From 6 April 2027, points-based late-submission penalties apply, with a £200 financial penalty once the points threshold is reached — [SJC Chester](https://www.sjc-chester.com/post/making-tax-digital-first-quarterly-deadline-passes-as-hmrc-urges-taxpayers-to-act); policy background at [GOV.UK: MTD for Income Tax and penalty reform](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/making-tax-digital-for-income-tax-and-penalty-reform) (not fetched).
- Earlier practitioner readiness: 34% of accountants said they were not prepared for April 2026; 65% lacked confidence in MTD requirements — [AccountingWEB "65% of accountants lack confidence in MTD requirements"](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/tax/personal-tax/65-of-accountants-lack-confidence-in-mtd-requirements) **[snippet]**. AccountingWEB 2025 Readiness Survey ("hundreds of responses"): 85% of firms in planning or pre-planning, 62% say clients not ready for quarterly submissions, nearly two-thirds had not finalised software, 76% unsure how to charge — **[snippet]** via the same search; exact URL for the readiness survey not confirmed.

### Inferences
- Wave 1 filing rate: 436k ÷ ~864k ≈ 50% filed on time; 436k ÷ 570k ≈ 76% of registrants filed. With no penalties in year one, late filers face little pressure until April 2027. That is when both penalties and wave 2 (~1.08m) arrive together, so **April–August 2027 is the likely capacity crunch**.
- Auto-enrolment of ~290k will push a late, unplanned batch of clients onto agents during autumn/winter 2026, overlapping with the January SA peak.

### Gaps
- No HMRC figure for second-quarter (7 Nov 2026) filings yet — the date has not arrived.
- I did not read an HMRC primary press release with the 570k/436k figures. All figures come via press quoting HMRC.

## 4. Number of UK accountancy practices by size

### Takeaway
About 34,000 accountancy businesses are supervised for anti-money-laundering (AML) by professional bodies: 60% firms (~20,600) and 40% sole practitioners (~13,700). ICAEW alone supervises ~9,500 firms. HMRC also supervises unaffiliated accountancy service providers, who are not counted in the 34k.

### Cited Findings
- **[primary, snippet]** HM Treasury AML/CTF supervision annual report 2024-25 (Dec 2025): 34,352 accountancy businesses supervised by Professional Body Supervisors, 60% firms and 40% sole practitioners — [GOV.UK annual report PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/693308125b5198836f304150/24-25_Annual_Report_.pdf) (figures from search summary; PDF not opened).
- ICAEW supervises ~9,500 member firms for AML; 1,185 monitoring reviews in 2024/25 — [ICAEW AML Supervision Report 2024/25](https://www.icaew.com/about-icaew/news/2025-news-releases/icaew-publishes-aml-supervision-report-2024-25-october-2025) **[snippet]**.

### Inferences
- If ~1.9m MTD taxpayers are agent-represented by 2028, spread over ~34k PBS-supervised businesses (plus HMRC-supervised ASPs), the average is ~55 MTD clients per business. Distribution is highly skewed; this is arithmetic, not data.
- Nearly every general-practice firm with Self Assessment clients will have some MTD clients by 2027. I found no number for firms that actually do.

### Gaps
- No ONS/ICAEW/ACCA breakdown by staff-size band (e.g., 2–5, 6–20 partners) was retrieved.
- Number of HMRC-supervised accountancy service providers (unaffiliated bookkeepers and accountants): **not found** in this pass.
- Number of agents registered for MTD ITSA / HMRC Agent Services Account counts: **not found**.

## 5. Capacity and staff shortage

### Takeaway
The best 2026 survey (Advancetrack, May 2026) says 73% of UK accounting firms turn away potential clients for lack of staff, and 69% have service lines at or near full capacity. MTD for Income Tax is explicitly named as a workload driver. The methodology (sample size) was not visible.

### Cited Findings
- **[vendor]** Advancetrack 2026 Accounting Talent Index (published ~May 2026): 73% of UK accounting firms turning away potential clients due to staff shortage; the same share describe the impact as "severe"; 74% think sustained workloads could push people out of the profession; 69% say service lines are at or near full capacity; 71% say growth is slowed by recruitment; 70% name talent shortage as a major barrier to expansion. MTD for Income Tax, the IHT relief cap, FRS 102 revisions and mandatory tax-adviser registration are cited as raising workloads — [Accountancy Today, 13 May 2026](https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/2026/05/13/accountancy-firms-turn-away-work-amid-staffing-crisis-survey-finds/); [Consultancy.uk](https://www.consultancy.uk/news/44502/three-in-four-accounting-firms-struggle-to-take-on-new-work-amid-hiring-challenges); [Advancetrack report page](https://www.advancetrack.com/talent/); [TaxCalc blog](https://www.taxcalc.com/blog/uk-accounting-talent-shortage-2026?hs_amp=true). All **[snippet]**; Accountancy Daily returned 403. **Bias flag:** Advancetrack sells outsourced accounting services, so this survey supports its own product.
- Commentary on the recruitment crisis — [Tax Research UK, 20 May 2026](https://www.taxresearch.org.uk/Blog/2026/05/20/there-is-a-recruitment-crisis-in-accounting-and-its-all-the-professions-own-fault/); [Accountancy Age, 17 Jul 2026](https://accountancyage.com/2026/07/17/rethinking-the-practice-model-for-a-new-generation-of-accountants/) (not fetched).
- **[snippet, vendor-cited]** "81.7% of accountants feel MTD is their biggest challenge in 2025" (attributed to an Accountex survey) — via [AccountingWEB industry-insights post on MTD outsourcing](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/community/industry-insights/mtd-outsourcing-why-smart-firms-are-outsourcing-making-tax-digital). ## 6. Evidence that practices already outsource

### Takeaway
Outsourcing is common, but the hard numbers are weak. Widely quoted figures ("68% of UK practices outsource", "79% outsourcing MTD compliance") come from outsourcer marketing blogs without traceable methodology. Treat them as claims, not measurements.

### Cited Findings
- **[vendor]** "Nearly 68% of UK accounting practices now utilise some form of outsourcing" — [Corient Business Solutions blog](https://corientbs.co.uk/blog/why-uk-accounting-firms-are-choosing-outsourced-bookkeeping-in-2025/) (snippet; no methodology seen).
- **[vendor]** "79% of those surveyed … are outsourcing MTD compliance to trusted outsourcing partners" — [AccountingWEB industry-insights post, "MTD Outsourcing: Why Smart Firms Are Outsourcing…"](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/community/industry-insights/mtd-outsourcing-why-smart-firms-are-outsourcing-making-tax-digital) (vendor-written sponsored post; survey source unclear; the figure is implausibly high and should not be used without the original).
- **[vendor]** "MTD has become one of the most common reasons firms make the first call to consider outsourced compliance" — surfaced via [acobloom](https://www.acobloom.com/blog/accounting-outsourcing-trends-uk/) / [mtd.digital practice guide](https://mtd.digital/mtd-income-tax/mtd-for-accountants-practice-guide/) search results (unattributed claim).
- **[snippet]** An ICAEW survey reportedly found ~62% of UK firms planned to increase reliance on outsourced *audit* services over three years — cited by a vendor blog in search results; original not located. It concerns audit, not MTD.
- A visible market of white-label/offshore suppliers targets UK practices explicitly for MTD (Corient, Acenteus, QX, Outbooks, Staxx, Advancetrack) — e.g. [Corient top-10 tax outsourcing list](https://corientbs.co.uk/blog/top-10-tax-outsourcing-companies-uk-2025/), [QX ROI guide](https://qxaccounting.com/uk/blog/accounting-outsourcing-cost-roi-guide), [Staxx](https://staxxsolutions.co.uk/why-uk-accounting-practices-are-losing-clients-before-mtd-even-starts/).

### Inferences
- The existence of many competing suppliers is the strongest evidence that practices buy this, and also evidence of competition. It is not proof of volume.
- No independent figure shows what share of practices outsource MTD quarterly work specifically.

### Gaps
- A credible (professional-body or methodology-disclosed) share of UK practices that outsource any work, by task and trend: **not found**.
- Specific measured statements about outsourcing MTD quarterly updates: **not found** beyond vendor claims.

## 7. Accountant sentiment (forums, community)

### Takeaway
Forum sentiment centres on how to price quarterly work and whether low-fee clients are worth keeping. Some plan to roughly double fees; some argue agents should not file quarterly updates at all. Sources are threads, not samples.

### Cited Findings
- AccountingWEB Any Answers "MTD Pricing" thread — practitioners discussing fee uplifts, some planning to roughly double fees for quarterly clients — [AccountingWEB](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/any-answers/mtd-pricing-0) **[snippet]**.
- "MTD for IT – an opportunity or a threat" thread — [AccountingWEB](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/any-answers/mtd-for-it-an-opportunity-or-a-threat) (not fetched).
- A blog by Amy Chin argues agents should be banned from submitting MTD quarterly updates (i.e., clients should self-file) — [AccountingWEB blog](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/community/blogs/amy-chin/ban-agents-from-submitting-mtd-quarterly-updates) (title only).
- "MTD: a perfect storm for accountants & bookkeepers" — [AccountingWEB industry insights](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/community/industry-insights/mtd-a-perfect-storm-for-accountants-bookkeepers) (vendor post, not fetched).

### Inferences
- There is real disagreement within the profession over whether agents should do quarterly updates at all. That matters for a back-office service: some firms will push clients to self-file instead of outsourcing.
